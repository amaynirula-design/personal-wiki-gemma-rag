# Elevator Cab Weight Failure

This note details an instance where a design decision at Otis Elevator Company led to an elevator cab exceeding its maximum weight limit due to failing to follow standard operating procedures. The owner acknowledged the mistake, worked with engineering and the client to redesign the cab, and implemented a pre-fabrication checklist to prevent a repeat.

## Key points

- Received critical feedback after a design decision did not follow standard operating procedures ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]])
- Requested premium customizations, including heavy wood wall panels and Italian marble flooring inside the elevator cab ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]], [[raw/Tanium Interview Prep.docx|Tanium prep]])
- Made a judgment call without completing the full SOP verification and approved the design for fabrication without formally verifying the final cab weight ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]], [[raw/Tanium Interview Prep.docx|Tanium prep]])
- Elevator cab was over the maximum weight limit, which meant that it would fail final state inspections ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]])
- Worked with engineering and the client to redesign the cab, ultimately replacing the marble flooring with thin-set tiles to bring the cab back within weight limits ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]], [[raw/Tanium Interview Prep.docx|Tanium prep]])
- Redesign caused project delays and resulted in financial losses for Otis ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]], [[raw/Tanium Interview Prep.docx|Tanium prep]])
- Created a pre-fabrication checklist that highlighted the most common pitfalls, shared it with other project managers ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]], [[raw/Tanium Interview Prep.docx|Tanium prep]])
- Since implementing this checklist, there has not been a repeat issue, and it has been adopted informally by peers as a final quality check before fabrication release ([[raw/Amazon Interview.docx|Amazon prep]])

## Where the sources differ

- The Amazon prep frames it as critical feedback and adds that the cab would have failed final state inspection and that peers informally adopted the checklist; the Tanium prep is a shorter outline dated 'last year'.
